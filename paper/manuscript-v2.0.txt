# Chapter 1
## Research design

At the end of a crash, a performing loan is still a claim on its borrower. Treating its collateral as an additional sale in a closing auction can drive down the price obtained by genuinely distressed loans. This paper asks how much the choice of liquidation clock, oracle mark and pool refill changes lender shortfall once the closing sale is confined to accounts whose remaining collateral is worth less than their debt.

The October 2026 capture covers ten Morpho vaults. The full capture reconciles integer borrow shares across 51 markets. The price replays use the ten books with WBTC, cbBTC or WETH collateral and USDC or USDT debt. This restriction gives each book an observed BTC or ETH price path and avoids assuming that principal tokens and dollar-linked collateral fall with BTC. The largest captured WBTC/USDC book is the anchor for the execution tests. A separate 2025 exercise uses the same market ID and the borrower balances that actually entered that day. [1] [2] [13]

Perez et al. and Qin et al. examine the operation and incentives of DeFi liquidations. Gudgeon et al. study lending solvency under liquidity stress. The contribution here is to compare the execution choices on fixed borrower books, audit the assumed depth curve against quotes, and compare the engine with a day of observed repayment data. The archive contains two earlier exercises on Compound repayments and a principal-token unwind. [3] [4] [5] [6]

# Chapter 2
## Observed depth and price paths

The direct-pool scan queries four Uniswap v3 fee tiers at each market's recorded block. A quote qualifies if a collateral sale produces positive loan-token proceeds and reaches the specified marginal price limit. The scan finds qualifying 2% quotes in 23 of the 51 captured markets. Eight of the ten replayed books have a matching direct-pool sensitivity. [2] [7]

At block 26,166,103, the anchor's 0.30% pool quoted about 683,889 USDC for a 2% marginal move. Matching that point gives a constant-product equivalent USDC reserve of 68.05 million. Direct Uniswap v3 quotes give a floor on gross proceeds for liquidators who can search other routes at that state. Pool balances, ticks and marginal prices vary between routes. Gas and competition for inclusion affect what liquidators actually realise. The appendix compares the constant-product curve with same-block quotes at larger moves and includes a two-hop and split-route benchmark. [2] [7]

I select four disjoint 30-day windows by the largest decline of the BTC/USD daily low relative to the origin close between 2018 and 2025. The replay uses the BTC and ETH hourly prices observed on those dates. Each path starts from its own origin close. Daily closes reconstructed from the hourly feeds agree with the retained daily closes. The additional low-and-close clock places the observed low before the close within each hour. It is a sensitivity for missed threshold crossings. [8]

# Chapter 3
## Validation of 30-day barrier forecasts

At the 15% closing-price barrier, EWMA Student t forecasts imply about 110 hits and the holdout records 101. This is good aggregate agreement for that threshold. The test has 702 overlapping daily origins with outcomes in 2024 and 2025, equivalent to about 23 disjoint 30-day windows by duration. Even disjoint windows can share a persistent volatility state. Agreement in total counts does not establish calibration at individual origins. [8] [9]

The first forecast uses information through 31 December 2023. Later forecasts update the variance state using returns known at the origin. The pre-test event frequency is a constant reference. Five-day blocks resample volatility-filtered returns and carry each daily low excursion with its close. The GJR-GARCH t fit targets the pre-test unconditional variance and permits persistence up to one. Its solution is 0.995 with essentially zero asymmetry. Mean reversion remains in the fit, though the estimated variance half-life is about 139 days. It is a near-integrated challenger to EWMA with little evidence here for a separate negative-return term. The filtered blocks preserve some historical extremes. The parametric specifications lack an explicit jump process. [9] [10]

# Chapter 4
## Liquidation and terminal recovery

All balances are in loan-token units. Let D be a borrower's debt and C its collateral marked at the opening price. At a decision check, O is the oracle price divided by its opening mark, S is the corresponding spot ratio, and the market's liquidation threshold is lambda. The simulator tries the most indebted accounts per unit of collateral first. A liquidator repays R and receives collateral with opening value I times R divided by O, subject to the remaining collateral and debt. The continuous approximation uses the Morpho incentive multiplier I. [12]

MATH liquidation_eligibility
D>\lambda C O,\qquad I=\frac{1}{0.7+0.3\lambda}
ENDMATH

For an inventory-free pool with loan reserve Y, the marginal net proceeds per unit repaid start at M. As liquidators sell, the available proceeds fall. Continuing until that marginal multiple reaches one gives the total profitable repayment budget B. A cap can reduce this budget further. The pool re-anchors to the incoming spot mark at each check. Any previous sales carried into that check reduce the new budget. The daily refill removes the chosen fraction of net inventory at the start of each new day. [2]

MATH daily_sale_budget
M=(1-f)IS/O,\qquad B=Y\,\frac{\sqrt{M}-1}{M}\quad(M>1)
DEFINITIONS f is the pool fee. The budget is zero when M is at or below one.
ENDMATH

At the horizon, I count interim bad debt already written off. I then test each remaining borrower at the terminal spot price. When the collateral covers the debt, the claim is carried at par and the account sends zero collateral to the pool. Each borrower below par sells its full remaining collateral. Let Q be the sum of the collateral units those borrowers sell. Let X and Y be the pool reserves after accounting for earlier net sales and before this batch. The batch gives each seller the same net price per unit, p. On a solvent book without interventions, both closing conventions give zero loss.

MATH closing_execution_price
p=\frac{(1-f)Y}{X+(1-f)Q}
DEFINITIONS Q includes only positions for which terminal spot value is below debt. Each distressed borrower receives p times the collateral it contributes.
ENDMATH

The flat comparison uses the same distress test and credits terminal spot less the pool fee, with no sale impact. In both cases any unpaid amount belongs to the lender of that borrower. Net creditor recovery is opening debt less the relevant loss. It includes interim repayments, repayments from outside borrower cash where allowed, and the closing value of unpaid claims.

## Conditional shortfall

### WBTC/USDC anchor

The entries below average four stress windows with equal weights. The strongest headline that survives both measures is an improvement from checking hourly under full replenishment. Loss falls by 78 basis points under the flat recovery rule and 145 basis points with the distressed-only pool batch. The pool result depends on size and replenishment. With 25% daily refill the prompt hourly policy improves on its no-liquidation control by 449 basis points. With no refill the gain is 46 basis points. When an account is already insolvent, the positive difference between the spot value of seized collateral and the payment received comes out of creditor recovery. The loss calculation already incorporates that transfer through the remaining collateral and debt balances. Subtracting the premium again would double-count it. The lagged policy pays about 165 basis points of this premium, compared with 58 for prompt hourly checks. On a rebound, a stale low oracle mark can increase sale proceeds per unit repaid and allow the simulator to retire more debt ahead of the closing batch. The lag improves on prompt hourly execution by 9 basis points under flat recovery and 583 under pool recovery. The larger pool advantage reflects the change in the volume of distressed debt and collateral left for that terminal sale. [2]

# Chapter 6
## Historical event comparison

The event window is 10 October 2025 UTC. A local Anvil fork was pinned to block 23,543,615 before the window. The state reads agree with the archive on the market totals, the oracle mark and a sample of borrower positions. To reconstruct the opening book, I collect historical Borrow log identities and read their positions at that block. The 362 debt balances account for every borrow share in the market. The initial balances include accrued interest at the opening timestamp. The engine reads the frozen book, sampled on-chain oracle state and completed Bitstamp minute closes. Spot changes are measured relative to the opening exchange print. The pool calibration uses the pre-event quote. [12] [13]

The records for the full day show 11 borrowers liquidated, repayments of 4,853,653 USDC and 0 USDC of protocol bad debt. There are 287 recorded oracle samples including the opening and closing blocks. I keep the engine's same-day write-offs next to protocol bad debt. The expected value of unresolved closing claims is a different quantity and has its own column. [13]

Case | Borrowers | Repaid USDC | Write-off USDC | Closing loss USDC
On-chain events | 11 | 4,853,653 | 0 | —
Direct pool, open depth | 11 | 3,889,552 | 0 | 0

Unbounded execution | 11 | 3,889,552 | 0 | 0
ENDTABLE

Table sources: [13]. The replay keeps the opening borrower book fixed except for its own liquidations. The hourly-refill case realigns the pool after every twelve sampled steps.

The open-depth replay predicted 11 borrowers and captured 9 of the 11 observed liquidations by borrower. It repaid 80.1% of the recorded amount and matched zero realised write-offs. The repayment and borrower sets are identical at hourly refill and unbounded depth, so depth cannot explain the repayment gap in this replay. The fixed book, price sampling and modelled repayment sizes are remaining sources of error. With zero realised bad debt, this day tests borrower selection and execution volume but provides no positive write-off event against which to assess loss severity.

# Chapter 7
## Scope and replication

The stress experiment holds the captured book fixed, except for modelled liquidations and the borrower-rescue policy. The rescue sensitivity allows outside repayment up to 5% of an account's opening debt. Reserves, oracle timing and transaction size are varied independently across specified policies. Executable liquidity can depend on routing, centralised venues and other orders. The historical engine misses intra-block state updates, later borrower flows and the sizes liquidators choose. Forecasts of the onset of a crash and recoveries conditional on one answer different questions.

The repository retains the borrower packets, chain inputs, price observations, held-out forecasts and policy grid. Running python research/reproduce_study.py regenerates the results from those inputs. The archived v1.2 experiment used a full-book terminal sale. That convention is superseded for lender shortfall.
