# Chapter 1
## Research question and design

A DeFi lending position joins a valuation rule to a sequence of transactions. The rule determines when collateral supports borrowing and when liquidation becomes permissible. The transaction sequence determines which assets can be released, sold and delivered to the creditor. A useful risk calculation follows both through the same balance sheet.

This paper examines the relationship with public evidence from Fulcrum, my dependency-analysis project. The empirical work combines a reconciled cross-section of vault borrower records, an independently extracted historical liquidation ledger, daily return-model evaluation and a counterfactual execution experiment at an identified Ethereum state. The reproducibility bundle contains the raw inputs and the scripts that produce the reported results. [1] [2]

The opening ledgers come from ten retained Morpho vault captures. The sample contains 51 positive-allocation markets and 3,547 borrower-market observations. The dynamic experiment uses one WBTC/USDC market with 614 borrowers. The execution case studies one public PT-sUSDe borrowing account at a pinned Ethereum block. The Compound extraction covers the block window named by the compensation proposal. These choices support mechanism and accounting comparisons on observed positions. They leave protocol-wide loss rates and general route capacity outside the scope of the evidence.

### Related work

Perez et al. measure liquidation exposure, incentive changes and liquidator response on Compound through September 2020. The event reconstruction here examines a later cDAI liquidation window and reconciles the resulting ledger to the proposed compensation by address. Qin et al. compare liquidation designs across major lending protocols and show how discounted collateral affects the borrower and the liquidator. This study tracks a different quantity through the same transaction: how much loan-token debt can be recovered after seizure and disposal. Gudgeon et al. stress-test a stylised lending protocol under impaired liquidity. The policy experiment below uses that stress-testing perspective with a reconciled account ledger and reports the dependence of its results on assumed depth. [21] [22] [23]

### Accounting and the estimand

For a terminal collateral recovery, account-level shortfall is the positive part of debt minus available proceeds. Market loss is obtained by summing those account shortfalls before applying a supplier's ownership fraction. This recourse boundary is central when well-collateralised borrowers share a lending market with borrowers whose cushions have been exhausted.

MATH paper_account_recovery
L_m=\sum_i\max(0,D_{im}-R_{im})
L_{\mathrm{holder}}=\theta\sum_m\omega_m L_m
DEFINITIONS D_im is debt of borrower i in market m, R_im creditor-accessible recovery, omega_m the vault's supplier ownership fraction in that market, and theta the holder's vault ownership fraction.
ENDMATH

A dynamic experiment replaces terminal proceeds with a path of repayments, collateral seizures and writeoffs. Its estimand is expected creditor loss conditional on the opening ledger, fitted return process and specified intervention policy. Oracle delay, loan-token reserves and repayment throughput enter that policy explicitly.

Comparisons use common generated paths. For each path, the simulator applies another capacity or recognition rule to the same opening positions and price history. This holds the market disturbance fixed while tracing the contractual consequence of the rule being changed. A paired standard error reports the numerical precision of each comparison.

The calibrated parameters describe return distributions. Capacity and disposal costs are controlled experimental inputs, whose range shows how the same market process can produce another credit outcome. The transaction case complements that abstraction by running a declared route through historical contract state and recording the first constraint reached.

### Evaluation and replication

The forecast comparison rewards the probability assigned to subsequent observed returns and examines crossings of one and five per cent lower-tail forecasts. The liquidation model is checked against accounting conservation and hand-calculable boundary paths. The execution model is checked through actual local EVM receipts and post-transaction balances.

These checks target different components of the analysis. Accurate price-density forecasts strengthen the market input; reconciled shares strengthen the opening loan book; completed transaction receipts strengthen the operational sequence. Together they support an investigation in which each reported number can be traced to an observation, an estimated parameter or an explicit experimental control.

The following sections develop the dataset, estimation and results before returning to the historical event and execution cases. The accompanying book expands the underlying accounting, protocol mechanics and historical context. This working paper concentrates on the empirical measurements and the computations needed to reproduce them.


# Chapter 2
## Data, selection and borrower accounting

Fulcrum's retained vault packets contain the information needed to move from a mechanism example to a borrower-level calculation. The sample covers ten named vaults observed during a short interval on 11 October 2026. Their allocations connect the holder to markets whose loans have different collateral cushions, sizes and account boundaries. Reconstructing those loans is the first part of the empirical study; applying the fitted price process is the second. [2]

The source file records 71 market identifiers across the vault configurations. Restricting the study to positive supply allocations leaves 51 markets, with 3,547 borrower-market positions and 3,059 unique addresses. Some addresses borrow in several markets, and several vaults supply the same market. The dataset consequently preserves both market identity and account identity throughout aggregation.

### Selection, duplication and reconciliation

For a repeated market, a complete borrower capture takes precedence over an incomplete one; among qualifying records the earliest capture is selected. This gives each underlying market one canonical loan book. Vault allocations retain their own recorded supplies and observation times, allowing the same market loss to pass into each supplier according to its claim.

The selection rule matters financially. Combining borrower rows from repeated captures would create fictitious additional loans. Treating repeated borrowers across independent markets as one cross-collateralised account would give creditors access to collateral they may have no right to seize. A position's analytical key is therefore the pair of market identifier and borrower address.

Borrow-share totals provide a direct reconciliation. For every selected market, summing captured borrower shares equals the recorded total borrow shares exactly. Pagination completeness flags agree with that result. Asset quantities require a further convention because interest changes the asset value represented by a share, and conversions round at the account level.

MATH empirical_share_coverage
\kappa_m=\frac{\sum_i b_{im}}{B_m}
\kappa_m=1\qquad\text{for all selected markets}
DEFINITIONS b_im is borrower i's recorded borrow-share quantity in market m, and B_m is that market's recorded total borrow shares. Reconciliation is performed on integer shares.
ENDMATH

The capture interval runs from 02:23:31.562 to 02:23:57.132 UTC. Indexer observations within the packets carry their own blocks and timestamps. The dataset is an asynchronous historical inventory, suitable for the declared balance-sheet experiment. The execution study uses direct common-block contract reads for the tighter tolerances of transaction execution.

This is a purposive sample of retained vaults, selected through Fulcrum's project work. Its reconciled accounting supports calculations for those observations. Estimating the prevalence of a feature across all DeFi would require a dated sampling universe and a selection design that represents that universe.

# Chapter 3
## Return distributions, estimation and forecast evaluation

A lending position converts an asset-price path into a sequence of permissions and payments. Estimating the distribution of that path supplies one input to the investigation, and the quality of the estimate can be tested before it is used to produce a credit-loss number. The estimation follows that order: construct a dated return sample, choose a volatility specification, estimate its parameters, evaluate predictions on later observations, and carry the resulting process into a position model.

The data are Fulcrum's retained Bitstamp BTC/USD and ETH/USD daily closing series, captured on 11 October 2026. The completed study window is 1 January 2018 through 31 December 2025. The raw file and clean return table accompany the text, together with the estimation script and numerical results. Each exchange timestamp identifies the start of a daily UTC candle; its closing value becomes available at the following midnight. Dates below use the candle's bucket label. Checks establish unique timestamps, consecutive daily spacing and positive closes. [3] [2]

For both assets, the sample contains 2,922 log returns. Training uses 1,461 returns from 2018-2021. Validation uses 730 returns from 2022-2023 to choose a volatility-decay parameter. Test evaluation uses 731 returns from 2024-2025. Once the test begins, the fitted distribution remains fixed while the volatility state updates from observations already received. This makes each test forecast an assessment of information available before the corresponding return.

### Returns and the quantity being estimated

A log return is the difference between consecutive log prices. Adding daily log returns gives the log price ratio over a longer holding period, which makes the same representation convenient for both estimation and simulation.

MATH empirical_returns
r_t=\log(P_t/P_{t-1})
P_{t+H}/P_t=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{H}r_{t+j}\right)
DEFINITIONS P_t is the daily closing price in dollars per unit of the underlying. The index t follows the exchange's daily candle order. H is the horizon in days.
ENDMATH

The fitted object is a conditional distribution of daily log returns. The credit experiment later holds the borrower ledger fixed at its observation date and applies generated price paths to that ledger. Its loss distribution therefore depends on both a statistical return model and a declared liquidation policy. Identifying those inputs at the start gives the resulting probability a financial meaning.

The sample shows substantial departures from a constant normal distribution. Pearson kurtosis is approximately 19.35 for BTC and 14.64 for ETH, against three for a normal distribution. Lag-one correlations of squared returns are approximately 0.095 and 0.124. These sample statistics describe heavy realised tails and persistence in return magnitude. A run of large changes carries information about the scale of tomorrow's change even when the direction is difficult to predict.

Conditional heteroskedastic models were developed to represent this changing scale. The ARCH literature and conditionally Student-t extensions provide the statistical background for the simple benchmark used here. The implementation keeps the filter small enough for the reader to reproduce every forecast and trace its effect on a liquidation boundary. [4] [5]

### A scale filter and a unit-variance innovation

The exponential filter assigns declining weights to earlier squared returns. Its decay parameter controls how quickly a disturbance changes subsequent forecasts. With a decay close to one, a single large observation enters gradually and remains influential for longer.

MATH empirical_ewma
h_{t+1}=\lambda h_t+(1-\lambda)r_t^2
r_t=\sqrt{h_t}\,z_t
\mathbb{E}[z_t]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[z_t^2]=1
DEFINITIONS h_t is the variance forecast formed before return r_t arrives. The decay parameter satisfies 0 < lambda < 1. Innovations z_t have mean zero and variance one.
ENDMATH

Initial variance is the training sample's mean squared return. Validation selects the decay that gives the highest mean Gaussian predictive log score over a grid of 0.90, 0.94, 0.97, 0.98, 0.985, 0.99, 0.9925, 0.995, 0.997, 0.999, 0.9995 and 0.9999. The grid includes finer steps near the original upper boundary of 0.99. All choices use the 2022–2023 validation period.

Asset | Decay | Tail nu | Start scale | Half-life, days
BTC | 0.985 | 3.151 | 2.05% | 45.9
ETH | 0.970 | 3.888 | 2.57% | 22.8
ENDTABLE

Table sources: [2].

The BTC maximum occurs at 0.985, inside the expanded grid. Its mean validation score is 2.15539 nats per day, compared with 2.15520 at 0.99 and 2.14042 at 0.995. The first pair is close. ETH selects 0.970, with 0.980 close behind. The selected decays imply half-lives of roughly 46 and 23 days for the squared-return weights. The start scale in the table refers to the October 2026 conditional simulation, after updating the variance on completed observations.

For the filtered Student-t model, the innovation is scaled to unit variance. If T has a standard Student-t distribution with degrees of freedom nu greater than two, multiplying T by the square root of (nu minus two) divided by nu gives the required innovation. This scaling keeps the variance forecast and simulation recursion in the same units.

MATH empirical_t_innovation
z_t=\sqrt{\frac{\nu-2}{\nu}}\,T_t,\qquad T_t\sim t_\nu
\operatorname{Var}(r_t\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})=h_t
DEFINITIONS nu controls tail thickness and is estimated on pre-test standardised returns. The information set contains returns observed before t. Innovations are independent in the specified simulation.
ENDMATH

The filtered tail parameter is fitted by conditional maximum likelihood using 2018-2023 observations after decay selection. The numerical search restricts nu to the interval from 2.05 to 100, enforcing finite innovation variance and allowing a nearly Gaussian upper end. Standardising a fitted t distribution after simulation would change its forecasts; the normalisation belongs inside estimation.

A large tail estimate has an immediate interpretation. Standardised observations remain heavy-tailed after the volatility filter has absorbed changing market scale. A small estimate puts appreciable probability on isolated large innovations. Parameter uncertainty then matters particularly for multi-day barrier events, because a single draw can dominate the path.

### The predictive density and its score

Four forecasts are evaluated: a zero-mean Gaussian with constant scale, a zero-location Student-t with constant scale, an EWMA Gaussian, and the EWMA Student-t. Constant-scale parameters use the whole pre-test period. Each zero-mean specification deliberately concentrates estimation on volatility and tails over the selected horizon.

A log score evaluates the density assigned to the realised return. Higher values reward a forecast that places more probability near observations it actually encounters. The change of scale from a standardised innovation to a return contributes a Jacobian term, which must enter the score.

MATH empirical_log_score
\ell_t=\log g\left(r_t/\sqrt{h_t}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log h_t
\overline{\ell}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^{N}\ell_t
DEFINITIONS g is the innovation density. Scores use natural logarithms and the same daily return units for every model. Constant-scale models use their corresponding constant h.
ENDMATH

A coverage count measures how often observations cross the forecast boundary. Clustering asks whether a crossing tends to be followed by another crossing. Financially, repeated misses during one volatile week can overwhelm a holder who could survive the same number of isolated misses scattered through two years. The forecast's timing therefore matters to the account's funding schedule.

The paired log-score comparison resamples circular blocks of thirty consecutive test observations. Keeping each pair of model scores together preserves their common realised return; keeping observations in blocks retains short-range dependence. The resulting interval describes uncertainty in the performance difference over that evaluation sample. It leaves parameter re-estimation and alternative historical regimes for further sensitivity work.

### Conditional simulation over thirty days

The forward experiment begins after the last completed candle available before the vault capture. Distribution parameters remain those fitted before the test, while completed 2026 observations update the variance state through the 10 October bucket. Starting variance thus uses data available to a researcher observing the vaults early on 11 October.

For each of 100,000 paths per asset, draw a standardised innovation, form a daily return, update price and carry the new squared return into the variance recursion. The next day's scale then depends on that path's own realised movement. This feedback lets a large simulated change produce a period of elevated volatility.

A comparison uses filtered historical simulation. It samples five-day circular blocks from centred, unit-variance pre-test innovations and applies them through the same variance recursion. Blocks retain local patterns from the innovation sample, while the Student-t specification permits innovations outside the observed sample's support. Both procedures begin at the same dated scale.

The barrier event is a closing price at least 35% below the path's initial price at some point during thirty days. It differs economically from a terminal decline: a position may cross a liquidation boundary and later recover. Retaining the whole path allows the next chapter to locate that crossing and follow subsequent debt payments.

Simulation error for a frequency is the square root of p times (1 minus p) divided by the number of paths. Those standard errors quantify variation from drawing a finite sample from the fitted model. Alternative return models, estimated parameters, daily observation resolution and the asset's actual trading environment contribute additional uncertainty. The comparison with historical resampling exposes one part of that specification sensitivity.

The model's conditional mean log return is zero. It serves a finite-horizon risk experiment, with creditor losses bounded by the debt ledger. An exponentiated Student-t log return has problematic positive price moments, so the study uses barrier frequencies and bounded credit-loss functions instead of expected spot prices or option values. That choice keeps the mathematical object aligned with the question being answered.

### Historical closing prices and intraday lows

Bitstamp's daily BTC/USD OHLC history supplies the low reached inside each UTC bucket. All 2,923 closes match the original closing-price series over the same dates. Starting with the 31 December 2017 close, each complete 30-day window compares two events relative to its origin close: a subsequent close at least 35% lower, and an intraday low at least 35% lower. The last window ends on 31 December 2025. [3] [24]

Among 2,893 overlapping origins, 123 cross at a close (4.25%) and 174 cross at an intraday low (6.01%). The additional 51 windows cross the threshold between closes. Contiguous hit windows form 9 runs by the close rule and 13 by the low rule. Overlap makes these descriptive frequencies across historical origins. The model's 0.193% BTC closing barrier instead conditions on the volatility state observed in October 2026. The older regimes in the historical average and the unobserved intraday sequence in the daily model both matter when applying these results to liquidations.

The original edition generated 50,000 price paths and used a 5,000-path subset for the more expensive borrower simulation. This edition uses all 100,000 generated BTC paths in both experiments, with the same draws across the liquidation policies.

# Chapter 4
## Liquidation with size-dependent execution

The liquidation experiment starts with the 614 borrowers in the selected WBTC/USDC Morpho market and 116.114 million USDC of opening debt. It fixes the WBTC/BTC basis and USDC unit and holds debt constant until repayment or writeoff. One hundred thousand daily BTC paths feed every policy. The oracle reads spot on the same day or with a one-day delay.

### Liquidator proceeds and the depth limit

Seized collateral has a spot value B_t per USDC of debt repaid. The liquidation incentive times the spot-to-oracle ratio determines B_t. A constant-product pool starts each day at the prevailing spot price with a loan-token reserve Y. After a fee f, a same-day cumulative sale with spot value X returns USDC according to the reserve curve. The associated selling discount rises with X. Here the pool resets each day and the experiment uses a stylised 1% acquisition and disposal wedge at zero size. Y measures assumed depth and is varied across policies. It is not a fit to a particular Curve or Pendle pool.

MATH pool_proceeds
B_t=\mathrm{LIF}\frac{S_t}{O_t}
U(X)=\frac{Y(1-f)X}{Y+(1-f)X}
DEFINITIONS S_t is relative spot, O_t the relative oracle price, Y the USDC reserve and f the proportional sale fee. X measures the marked spot value of seized collateral sold into the pool.
ENDMATH

Each additional unit of repayment increases X by B_t. Marginal rather than average sale proceeds determine whether that unit can pay for itself. The last profitable repayment occurs where the marginal proceeds reach one USDC per USDC of debt repaid. If even the first unit fails the test, the depth budget is zero.

MATH pool_repayment_budget
b_t=(1-f)B_t
M_t=\frac{Y}{b_t}\max\left(0,\sqrt{b_t}-1\right)
DEFINITIONS M_t caps aggregate profitable repayment given a finite reserve. The model assumes this volume passes through one pool and that successive units face the resulting price impact.
ENDMATH

Each day the engine orders eligible borrowers by loan-to-value and applies the smaller of M_t and any separately imposed daily repayment cap. Repayment cannot exceed debt or the amount supported by remaining collateral at the stated liquidation incentive. When seizure exhausts an account's collateral, residual debt enters writeoff. At the horizon, terminal account-level recoveries retain the same 1% haircut in every policy. This common salvage convention isolates the effect of the intervening liquidation policy.

MATH pool_credit_losses
C_{i,t+1}=C_{i,t}-R_{i,t}\mathrm{LIF}/O_t
D_{i,t+1}=D_{i,t}-R_{i,t}
L_H=W_H+\sum_i\max(0,D_{i,H}-C_{i,H}S_H(1-d))
DEFINITIONS C_it is collateral at the opening price, D_it remaining debt, R_it daily repayment, W_H cumulative writeoff and d the common terminal haircut.
ENDMATH

In the base pool, a matched spot and oracle price give a marginally profitable daily repayment budget of about 1,602,886 USDC. That lies above the 1% cap of about 1.16 million and below the 5% cap of about 5.81 million. As a result, uncapped throughput and the 5% cap give identical outcomes at zero lag. Raising the cap from 1% to 5% changes 440 paths and reduces mean loss by 284 USDC (paired SE 87). Against zero liquidation, the 5% cap reduces the positive-loss share from 4.834% to 1.423% and lowers mean loss by about 1,450 USDC (paired SE 343).

Shallower depth pushes mean loss toward the no-intervention case. Deeper reserves allow larger repayments before the marginal profitability limit binds. One-day oracle lag produces a mean increase of roughly 346 USDC against the no-liquidation control, with a paired SE of 401. Its sign is unresolved under simulation sampling error. The lag changes both eligibility and the economics of selling the collateral. Paths that jump directly into severe impairment still incur losses even when deep liquidity supports prompt liquidation.

### Interpretation

The original fixed-discount model was a benchmark. At 86% LLTV, a constant disposal discount above 4.2% switches off matched-spot liquidation by construction. The depth model gives the same economic condition at the margin and lets it emerge at different traded sizes. The reported policy gaps are conditional on the book, fitted prices, fee, and reserve assumptions. They quantify the relative importance of repayment budgets and assumed depth in this market. They do not estimate market depth or the causal effect of changing a live protocol's risk parameters.

### Reconciling the proposal's numbers

The proposal text reports 85,220,406.43 DAI repaid. [7] The extraction exceeds that figure by 2,068.083270326810927896 DAI, approximately 0.00243% of the independently extracted total. The published address-level distribution file provides a way to investigate the difference.

That file contains 124 addresses and compensation amounts summing to approximately 6,817,797.961061626 DAI. Dividing by the stated eight per cent factor implies approximately 85,222,474.513270325 DAI of repayments. # Chapter 7
## Interpretation and replication

The empirical results connect three levels of a position investigation. Borrower records locate the recourse boundary and distribute collateral cushions across accounts. A return model assigns probabilities to market paths and is evaluated on later observations. The transaction sequence determines whether a holder or liquidator can use the resources indicated by that path.

The Compound extraction demonstrates the value of checking an aggregate against its primary ledger. Repayments are concentrated in a few borrowers and a few liquidator addresses. The address-level compensation file agrees with the event extraction while the prose total differs. The resulting reconciliation is small in dollar scale but consequential for reproducibility: it identifies which source representation a later calculation should use.

The vault study demonstrates why account-level recovery is needed. Aggregate collateral can exceed aggregate debt while individual loans contribute shortfall. Applying supplier ownership after that calculation preserves the connection between a holder and the creditor claim whose value is being estimated.

The forecast study gives the statistical input an empirical test. A volatility filter and scaled Student-t innovations improve held-out predictive density relative to the fixed Gaussian benchmark in the selected sample. Tail counts provide another view of the same forecasts, while the block-resampled score comparison measures uncertainty in their performance difference.

The daily simulation finds that the model's assumed depth can bind before a repayment cap does. The conversion from a market drawdown into lender shortfall depends on initial account cushions, liquidation eligibility and the marginal execution cost at the size traded. The experiments hold accounting and market draws fixed to expose those mechanisms. The result pertains to this one market.

The execution case shows the cost of decreasing exposure through a particular path. Marked equity declined as the health ratio rose. Larger tests located two different constraints for that path. General exit capacity would require original aggregator transaction data or further independently timestamped route and position studies.

### Practical use

An analyst applying the workflow begins with a dated ledger and a declared action. For a supplier, the calculation follows borrower shortfalls into the supplier's market share and then into holder ownership. For a financed borrower, it follows temporary funding into collateral release, disposal proceeds and residual obligations.

The required evidence becomes more demanding as the decision approaches a boundary. A broad portfolio screen can work with an explicitly timed inventory capture. An operation close to its health limit benefits from common-block reads, contract-scale rounding and a rehearsal of the exact route. The present study illustrates both levels and retains their records.

The standard models used here make the calculations accessible to inspection. Their empirical value comes from joining them to a reconciled account structure and a tested execution sequence. # Appendix A
## Statistical calculations and computational checks

The empirical chapters use a small set of probability calculations repeatedly. Writing their conventions explicitly makes the generated tables easier to reproduce and prevents a change of units or tail definition from changing an investment conclusion.

### The scaled Student-t density

For degrees of freedom nu greater than two, the innovation used in return-model section rescales a standard Student-t variable to variance one. Combining that innovation with the volatility state h gives the conditional return density below.

MATH appendix_t_density
f(r\mid h,\nu)=\frac{\Gamma((\nu+1)/2)}{\Gamma(\nu/2)\sqrt{(\nu-2)\pi h}}
\times\left(1+\frac{r^2}{(\nu-2)h}\right)^{-(\nu+1)/2}
DEFINITIONS Γ denotes the gamma function, h the conditional variance and nu the tail parameter. The displayed density is for zero conditional mean.
ENDMATH

The factor containing h is the change-of-units term. Removing it would score standardised innovations in one model and raw returns in another, altering the comparison as market volatility changes. The implementation evaluates the full return density for every test observation.

Unit variance also determines the simulation recursion. Conditional on h_t, the next squared return has expectation h_t, so the EWMA update has expected value h_t. For this finite-horizon specification, the variance state reacts to simulated observations while preserving that conditional expectation.

The fitted nu values allow finite second moments. Their implications for higher moments follow from the t distribution's tail order. The credit-loss experiment remains numerically integrable because each path's aggregate unpaid debt is bounded by the opening debt plus the assumed accrual over the horizon.

### Coverage as a likelihood calculation

For a lower-tail forecast q_t at probability alpha, define the hit indicator as one when the realised return falls below q_t. Correct unconditional coverage assigns hit probability alpha. The empirical hit frequency estimates that probability from the test sample.

MATH appendix_coverage
I_t=\mathbf{1}\{r_t<q_t\},\qquad x=\sum_t I_t
\widehat p=x/N
\mathrm{LR}_{\mathrm{uc}}=2\left[x\log\frac{\widehat p}{\alpha}+(N-x)\log\frac{1-\widehat p}{1-\alpha}\right]
DEFINITIONS N is the test sample size, x the exceedance count and alpha the forecast tail probability. Terms of the form zero times log zero use their limiting value zero.
ENDMATH

The statistic compares the likelihood at the forecast probability with the likelihood at the sample's fitted frequency. Its usual large-sample reference has one chi-squared degree of freedom. The companion reports the statistic alongside raw counts and exact binomial reference intervals, which remain easier to interpret when the nominal tail contains only a few observations. [19]

For an interior count, the two-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson interval uses the 2.5% quantile of a Beta(x, N minus x plus one) distribution and the 97.5% quantile of Beta(x plus one, N minus x). Zero hits set the lower endpoint to zero; N hits set the upper endpoint to one. These are reference intervals for an independent Bernoulli sampling model.

Serial dependence is examined through the transition counts n_00, n_01, n_10 and n_11. The fitted probability of a hit after a non-hit is n_01 divided by n_00 plus n_01; after a hit it is n_11 divided by n_10 plus n_11. A likelihood comparison with a common transition probability provides an independence diagnostic. A small tail sample limits the precision of both transition estimates. [20]

### Comparing two forecasting rules

Model scores share a realised return on each day. Their paired difference is therefore the natural observation for comparing performance. Circular block resampling draws a start point, takes thirty consecutive score differences with wraparound, repeats until it has a full-length sample and calculates its mean.

The reported 95% percentile interval uses 2,000 such resamples. This procedure carries local dependence into the comparison and conditions on the fitted forecasting rules. Re-estimating the rules inside each resample would address another component of uncertainty and would require repeating the complete chronological selection procedure.

### Expected shortfall with an atom at zero

Creditor-loss distributions often contain many zeros because collateral cushions absorb ordinary movements. Their value at risk can equal zero even while the worst paths contain substantial shortfall. Expected shortfall averages the specified upper probability mass.

MATH appendix_expected_shortfall
\operatorname{ES}_\alpha=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int_\alpha^1 Q(u)\,du
DEFINITIONS Q is the loss quantile function and alpha the tail boundary. For the 100,000-path experiment, the worst one per cent consists of the 1,000 largest losses.
ENDMATH

Selecting every observation greater than or equal to a zero quantile would include the entire zero-loss mass and change the statistic into an ordinary mean. The implementation sorts the simulated losses and averages the upper fifty. A fractional-mass convention would be needed when the chosen tail fraction times the path count is non-integer.

Monte Carlo standard error of a sample mean is the sample standard deviation divided by the square root of the path count. For a probability estimate it is the square root of p times (1 minus p) divided by that count. These calculations concern sampling from the declared simulation. Parameter uncertainty, alternative mechanisms and empirical coverage remain attached to their own evidence.

### Paired policy differences

Let L_A and L_B be the losses generated by two intervention rules on the same price path. Their difference is calculated path by path before averaging. The standard error then follows the variation of those differences, preserving the covariance supplied by their common market history.

MATH appendix_paired_mc
\widehat\Delta=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}(L_{Am}-L_{Bm})
\operatorname{SE}(\widehat\Delta)=\frac{s_{A-B}}{\sqrt{M}}
DEFINITIONS M is the number of simulated paths, and s_(A-B) the sample standard deviation of the paired loss differences.
ENDMATH

A comparison whose estimated effect is close to that error deserves additional paths or a coarser economic conclusion. In this study, the policy table therefore carries simulation uncertainty beside the estimated loss. The calculation makes the scale of the available evidence visible before the result enters an expected-cost decision.

### Reproducible computation

The companion keeps raw packets, request-response records, cleaned tables, fitted parameters, generated paths and final summaries. The raw manifest fingerprints the bytes analysed. Reproduction starts with the offline verification, then runs the model and liquidation scripts against the retained files.

The event decoder checks unique transaction-and-log keys and consecutive requested block coverage. The vault decoder reconciles integer borrow shares before producing floating-point stress calculations. The PT calculation preserves contract-scale integers and rounding directions. The fork helper restricts execution to its local chain identifier, and the driver sends state-changing calls only to its loopback endpoint.

Boundary checks exercise healthy loans, gradual and instantaneous declines, zero liquidation capacity and stale-price purchase economics. The executable cases are deliberately small enough to inspect by hand. They connect the larger simulation to the repayment and collateral equations on which its conclusions depend.
